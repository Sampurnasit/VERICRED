## 1. Domain Terminology

### Issuer
The legal or accredited educational entity (e.g., a university, college, or examination board) that asserts academic claims about a subject, signs the credential cryptographically, and anchors its status to the network.

### Holder
The individual (student, alumnus, certificate earner) who receives the verifiable credential from the issuer, retains sovereign custody of the credential in a digital wallet, and decides when and with whom to share presentations.

### Verifier
The entity (corporate recruiter, admissions officer, licensing board) that inspects a credential presentation or zero-knowledge proof to confirm its authenticity, integrity, issuer authorization, and active status.

### Verifiable Credential (VC)
A tamper-evident, cryptographically signed set of claims asserted by an issuer conforming to the W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0 specification.

### Verifiable Presentation (VP)
A package created by a holder from one or more verifiable credentials, tailored for a specific verifier, typically containing selective attribute disclosure and cryptographic proof of possession.

### Decentralized Identifier (DID)
A globally unique, cryptographically verifiable identifier that resolves without a centralized registration authority (e.g., `did:vericred:institution:rcciit`).

### Credential Commitment
A cryptographic hash value $C$ computed over private credential claims and a blinding salt:
$$C = \text{Poseidon}(x, s)$$
The commitment binds the claims irrevocably to the credential without revealing the underlying plaintext values.

### Digital Signature
A mathematical scheme demonstrating the authenticity of a digital message. In VERICRED, issuers sign credential commitments using EdDSA over the BabyJubjub twisted Edwards curve or standard ECDSA/Ed25519 suites.

### Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP)
A cryptographic protocol by which a prover demonstrates to a verifier that a statement is true (e.g., "my CGPA is at least 8.0") without revealing any secret information beyond the validity of the statement itself.

### Groth16
A pairing-based zk-SNARK proving system introduced by Jens Groth in 2016. It offers the smallest proof size (3 group elements, 128 bytes) and fastest verification time (a single multi-pairing) among existing zk-SNARK constructions, ideal for in-browser verification and smart contract execution.

### Poseidon
A cryptographic hash function designed specifically for zero-knowledge arithmetic circuits over prime fields. Poseidon minimizes algebraic constraint counts (R1CS) by up to $10\times$ compared to traditional hashes like SHA-256 or Keccak.

### BabyJubjub
A twisted Edwards elliptic curve defined over the scalar field of the BN254 (alt_bn128) curve. It enables highly efficient point operations and EdDSA signature verification inside Circom zk-SNARK circuits.

### Revocation
The formal process by which an issuing institution invalidates a previously issued credential due to academic retraction, administrative error, or disciplinary action. In VERICRED, revocations are immutably recorded in `RevocationRegistry.sol`.

### Selective Disclosure
The capability of a credential holder to reveal only specific desired attributes (e.g., degree name and graduation year) while completely withholding sensitive attributes (e.g., date of birth, home address, exact marks).

### Nonce
A cryptographic number used once (e.g., a 256-bit random integer generated by a verifier) included in a presentation to guarantee freshness and prevent replay attacks.

### Nullifier
A unique deterministic cryptographic value derived from a private credential secret and context identifier, used to track credential status or prevent double-spending without revealing the underlying credential identity.

### Issuer Registry
An on-chain smart contract directory (`InstitutionRegistry.sol`) that maintains the authorized state and verified public keys of accredited academic institutions.

### Fixed-Point Integer Representation
A numerical encoding scheme where floating-point decimal numbers are converted to integers by multiplying by a constant scale factor. In VERICRED, a CGPA of $8.72$ is scaled by $100$ to become the integer $872$, enabling deterministic arithmetic within finite field algebraic circuits.

### Rank-1 Constraint System (R1CS)
A system of mathematical equations of the form $(A \cdot w) \times (B \cdot w) = (C \cdot w)$ used to encode computational logic into constraint systems solvable by zk-SNARK proving schemes.

### Witness
The complete assignment of values to all input, intermediate, and output wires of a zero-knowledge circuit for a specific execution. In VERICRED, the private witness includes the student's exact scaled CGPA and random salt, while the public witness includes the commitment and eligibility threshold.
